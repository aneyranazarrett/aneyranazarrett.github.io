Teaching

I have taught at Harvard for over six years, across three schools and three audiences: working professionals in executive education courses at Harvard's Division of Continuing Education, professional master's students at the Harvard Kennedy School, and undergraduates at Harvard College. My teaching draws directly on my research — causal inference, quantitative analysis for decision-making, and survey design — applied to concrete organizational and policy problems.

A distinctive emphasis of my teaching is the data itself. My research is built on large, unstructured administrative records — payroll, biometric time-clocks, operations logs — and I teach students to turn such records into credible evidence. As AI makes analysis ever cheaper, the scarce skill in empirical research is understanding, structuring, and questioning the data.

Graduate — Harvard Kennedy School

Quantitative methods teaching and capstone advising for professional master's students.

  • Teaching Fellow, Advanced Quantitative Methods: Causal Inference (API-210) (2026) The modern causal-inference toolkit — randomized evaluations, instrumental variables, regression discontinuity, and difference-in-differences. Econometrics core for MPA/ID students.
  • Capstone advising: Policy Analysis Exercise (PAE, MPP) and Second-Year Policy Analysis (SYPA, MPA/ID) The degrees' required capstones: second-year students act as consultants to a real client organization, producing analysis and actionable recommendations on a live policy or management problem.

  • Teaching Fellow, The Politics of Development (DPI-410) (2021) State capacity, democracy, clientelism, and reform — how politics shapes growth and development. Required course for MPA/ID candidates.

  • Course Coach, Environmental Politics: Negotiation, Persuasion, and Leadership (IGA-455) (2021) Negotiation, persuasion, and advocacy skills built through simulations of real environmental conflicts. Elective for HKS master's students.
